# LESSON 100
**Are You Likeable?**

In the drawing room of an old estate, guests gathered beneath dim chandeliers, and the hostess moved among them with a **cordial** (adj.) smile, refilling glasses before anyone dared ask. Not everyone matched her warmth — a widow in grey stood **aloof** (adj.) near the window, arms folded, watching the room as though it belonged to someone else. Across the parlor, a stooped old man was whispered about as the household **misanthrope** (n.), a man rumored to loathe every soul who crossed his threshold, though the servants swore he wept in private. Beside him sat his nephew, so **congenial** (adj.) and so **amiable** (adj.) that strangers left his company feeling as though they'd known him for years. Yet not all in that house wore kindness so easily — the housekeeper found the master's tantrums **abominable** (adj.), his cruelty toward the staff genuinely **odious** (adj.), and privately called him nothing but an old **curmudgeon** (n.) behind his back.

**cordial** *(adj.)*
- Meaning: Polite and warm, eager to accommodate
- বাংলা অর্থ: সৌহার্দ্যপূর্ণ, আন্তরিক
- Feeling/Tone: Welcoming, gracious, a touch performative
- Texture: Smooth, warm like candlelight
- Usage: "She gave him a cordial welcome despite her private reservations."
- Etymology: Latin *cor* — "heart"
- Synonyms: gracious, affable, warm
- Antonyms: cold, hostile, aloof

**aloof** *(adj.)*
- Meaning: Distant, standoffish, emotionally removed
- বাংলা অর্থ: নির্লিপ্ত, দূরত্ব বজায় রাখা
- Feeling/Tone: Cool, guarded, quietly withholding
- Texture: Chilled, distant, wind-swept
- Usage: "He remained aloof at the party, speaking to no one."
- Etymology: Nautical origin — a ship steered away from shore, "into the wind"
- Synonyms: distant, reserved, detached
- Antonyms: cordial, sociable, warm

**misanthrope** *(n.)*
- Meaning: A person who hates humankind and avoids company
- বাংলা অর্থ: মানবতাবিদ্বেষী, মানুষবিদ্বেষী ব্যক্তি
- Feeling/Tone: Bitter, isolating, faintly tragic
- Texture: Hard, brittle, closed-off
- Usage: "The old hermit was branded a misanthrope by the villagers."
- Etymology: Greek *misos* ("hatred") + *anthropos* ("man")
- Synonyms: cynic, recluse, curmudgeon
- Antonyms: philanthropist, humanitarian

**congenial** *(adj.)*
- Meaning: Pleasant, friendly, and agreeable in nature or company
- বাংলা অর্থ: বন্ধুত্বপূর্ণ, সহৃদয়
- Feeling/Tone: Light, easy, welcoming
- Texture: Soft, comfortable, familiar
- Usage: "They found the new neighbor congenial from the very first meeting."
- Etymology: Latin *congenialis* — "of the same nature"
- Synonyms: amiable, affable, sociable
- Antonyms: disagreeable, hostile, odious

**amiable** *(adj.)*
- Meaning: Friendly, good-natured, and easy to like
- বাংলা অর্থ: বন্ধুভাবাপন্ন
- Feeling/Tone: Sunny, gentle, approachable
- Texture: Smooth, unforced, warm
- Usage: "His amiable manner put even strangers at ease."
- Etymology: Latin *amicabilis* — "friendly," from *amare* ("to love")
- Synonyms: congenial, genial, affable
- Antonyms: hostile, surly, curmudgeonly

**abominable** *(adj.)*
- Meaning: Causing moral revulsion; despicable
- বাংলা অর্থ: জঘন্য, ঘৃণ্য
- Feeling/Tone: Repelled, condemning, dark
- Texture: Rough, ugly, unbearable
- Usage: "The abominable treatment of the workers finally sparked outrage."
- Etymology: Latin *abominari* — "to deprecate as an ill omen"
- Synonyms: detestable, loathsome, odious
- Antonyms: admirable, delightful, amiable

**odious** *(adj.)*
- Meaning: Extremely unpleasant; deserving hatred
- বাংলা অর্থ: ঘৃণ্য, বিরক্তিকর
- Feeling/Tone: Repulsive, sharp, condemning
- Texture: Bitter, corrosive
- Usage: "His odious remarks emptied the room within minutes."
- Etymology: Latin *odium* — "hatred"
- Synonyms: repugnant, abominable, hateful
- Antonyms: pleasant, agreeable, congenial

**curmudgeon** *(n.)*
- Meaning: A grouchy, ill-tempered person, especially an older one
- বাংলা অর্থ: বদমেজাজি লোক
- Feeling/Tone: Gruff, comic-tinged, crusty
- Texture: Coarse, prickly
- Usage: "The old curmudgeon shooed away every child who rang his bell."
- Etymology: Origin uncertain; in use since 16th-century English
- Synonyms: grouch, misanthrope, crank
- Antonyms: charmer, sweetheart

# LESSON 101
**Hurtful Words**

The council chamber, once a place of measured debate, had turned into a battlefield of tongues. The elder statesman **lambasted** (v.) the young reformer before the entire assembly, his voice cracking like a whip across the marble hall. What began as policy disagreement grew **acrimonious** (adj.), each side trading barbs no longer meant to persuade but to wound. The newspapers, sensing blood, **excoriated** (v.) the reformer daily, peeling back his reputation layer by layer. Some called the elder's attacks **invidious** (adj.), designed less to win the argument than to breed envy and division among allies. Whispers followed — an **aspersion** (n.) cast here, a rumor planted there — until the reformer could no longer tell friend from saboteur. Colleagues who once supported him now chose to **rail** (v.) against his every proposal, and even the quietest among them began to **inveigh** (v.) against his character in private letters. By winter, the chamber's language had grown so **vituperative** (adj.) that the speaker himself called for a recess, fearing the room might combust.

**lambaste** *(v.)*
- Meaning: To criticize someone harshly and publicly
- বাংলা অর্থ: তীব্র সমালোচনা করা
- Feeling/Tone: Fierce, blunt, punishing
- Texture: Heavy, striking, blunt-force
- Usage: "The critic lambasted the film for its lazy script."
- Etymology: Old English *lam* ("to beat") + *baste*
- Synonyms: berate, excoriate, castigate
- Antonyms: praise, commend, laud

**acrimonious** *(adj.)*
- Meaning: Marked by bitterness or ill will in speech or manner
- বাংলা অর্থ: তিক্ত, কটু
- Feeling/Tone: Sour, hostile, sharp-edged
- Texture: Jagged, corrosive
- Usage: "The divorce proceedings turned acrimonious within the first hour."
- Etymology: Latin *acrimonia* — "sharpness, pungency"
- Synonyms: bitter, caustic, rancorous
- Antonyms: amicable, cordial, congenial

**excoriate** *(v.)*
- Meaning: To criticize someone severely; literally, to strip skin off
- বাংলা অর্থ: প্রচণ্ড সমালোচনা করা
- Feeling/Tone: Brutal, flaying, merciless
- Texture: Raw, stripped, painful
- Usage: "The press excoriated the official after the scandal broke."
- Etymology: Latin *excoriare* — "to strip off the hide"
- Synonyms: lambaste, castigate, flay
- Antonyms: praise, commend

**invidious** *(adj.)*
- Meaning: Causing resentment or envy; unfairly discriminating
- বাংলা অর্থ: ঈর্ষা বা বিদ্বেষ সৃষ্টিকারী
- Feeling/Tone: Sly, unjust, quietly corrosive
- Texture: Slippery, uneven, divisive
- Usage: "The manager's invidious comparisons pitted colleague against colleague."
- Etymology: Latin *invidia* — "envy"
- Synonyms: discriminatory, unjust, offensive
- Antonyms: fair, impartial, equitable

**aspersion** *(n.)*
- Meaning: A false or misleading accusation that damages reputation
- বাংলা অর্থ: চরিত্রহনন, কুৎসা
- Feeling/Tone: Sneaky, damaging, whispered
- Texture: Thin, seeping, poisonous
- Usage: "He cast aspersions on her honesty without a shred of proof."
- Etymology: Latin *aspergere* — "to sprinkle upon"
- Synonyms: slander, defamation, slur
- Antonyms: praise, compliment, tribute

**rail** *(v.)*
- Meaning: To complain or protest loudly and at length
- বাংলা অর্থ: তীব্র প্রতিবাদ করা
- Feeling/Tone: Loud, indignant, unrelenting
- Texture: Grating, forceful
- Usage: "She railed against the injustice for nearly an hour."
- Etymology: Old French *railler* — "to mock"
- Synonyms: inveigh, protest, fulminate
- Antonyms: praise, endorse

**inveigh** *(v.)*
- Meaning: To speak or write with strong hostility against something
- বাংলা অর্থ: কঠোর ভাষায় বিরোধিতা করা
- Feeling/Tone: Charged, condemning, formal-angry
- Texture: Sharp, verbal, cutting
- Usage: "The columnist inveighed against the new policy in print."
- Etymology: Latin *invehi* — "to attack with words"
- Synonyms: rail, denounce, censure
- Antonyms: endorse, commend

**vituperative** *(adj.)*
- Meaning: Bitter and abusive in language
- বাংলা অর্থ: গালিগালাজপূর্ণ, তিরস্কারমূলক
- Feeling/Tone: Venomous, scalding, unrestrained
- Texture: Harsh, jagged, burning
- Usage: "His vituperative outburst shocked even his closest allies."
- Etymology: Latin *vituperare* — "to blame, censure"
- Synonyms: abusive, scathing, vitriolic
- Antonyms: complimentary, gracious

# LESSON 102
**How Pure?**

The old temple had once stood **immaculate** (adj.), its white stones untouched by dust or sin, until the war brought soldiers who **defiled** (v.) its sacred halls with fire and boot-prints. Even the wells were **adulterated** (v.), poisoned with salt so nothing pure could be drawn from them again. Season by season, the town watched its former glory **degrade** (v.), its gardens choked with weeds, its statues cracked. Rumors began to **sully** (v.) the priest's name too, though no one could say why a man once thought **unblemished** (adj.) should now be doubted. Perhaps it was simply the times — an age that seemed determined to **vitiate** (v.) anything the world still called holy.

**immaculate** *(adj.)*
- Meaning: Perfectly clean; free from flaw or blemish
- বাংলা অর্থ: নিষ্কলঙ্ক, নিখুঁত
- Feeling/Tone: Pristine, reverent, untouched
- Texture: Smooth, gleaming, unmarked
- Usage: "The bride's dress was immaculate, without a single crease."
- Etymology: Latin *immaculatus* — "un-stained" (*macula* = "stain")
- Synonyms: pristine, spotless, flawless
- Antonyms: defiled, sullied, tainted

**defile** *(v.)*

- বাংলা অর্থ: অপবিত্র করা, কলুষিত করা
- Feeling/Tone: Violating, desecrating, grim
- Texture: Trampled, soiled
- Usage: "Vandals defiled the ancient burial ground."
- Etymology: Old French *defouler* — "to trample upon"
- Synonyms: desecrate, sully, pollute
- Antonyms: purify, sanctify, cleanse

**adulterate** *(v.)*

- Feeling/Tone: Corrupting, deceptive, degrading

**degrade** *(v.)*

- Feeling/Tone: Diminishing, sad, eroding
- Texture: Crumbling, worn-down
- Usage: "Years of neglect degraded the once-grand mansion."
- Etymology: Latin *de-* ("down") + *gradus* ("step")
- Synonyms: debase, deteriorate, diminish
- Antonyms: enhance, elevate, improve

**sully** *(v.)*
- Meaning: To damage the purity or reputation of something
- বাংলা অর্থ: কলঙ্কিত করা
- Feeling/Tone: Tarnishing, quiet, insidious

- Usage: "The scandal sullied his once-spotless reputation."
- Etymology: Old French *souiller* — "to soil"

- Antonyms: cleanse, honor, exalt

**unblemished** *(adj.)*
- Meaning: Without flaw or stain; perfect
- বাংলা অর্থ: নিখুঁত, দাগহীন
- Feeling/Tone: Pure, admired, intact
- Texture: Smooth, whole
- Usage: "Her unblemished record earned her the promotion."
- Etymology: *un-* + *blemish* (Old French *blesmir*, "to make pale")

**vitiate** *(v.)*
- Meaning: To spoil or impair the quality or effectiveness of something
- বাংলা অর্থ: নষ্ট করা, দূষিত করা
- Feeling/Tone: Undermining, corrosive, weakening

- Usage: "The forged signature vitiated the entire contract."
- Etymology: Latin *vitiare* — "to spoil, make faulty" (*vitium* = "fault")
- Synonyms: corrupt, contaminate, impair
- Antonyms: strengthen, validate, purify

# LESSON 103
**How Do I Express Myself?**

In the village square, an old philosopher held court every evening, dispensing wisdom to whoever would listen. His favorite **dictum** (n.) — that suffering teaches more than comfort ever could — was repeated by farmers and children alike. Yet not everything he said carried weight; on tired nights, he leaned on **platitude** (n.) instead, murmuring worn phrases that comforted without illuminating. His true students learned to prize instead his sharper **aphorisms** (n.), brief flashes of insight that could fit on a single breath. The young mocked the town's fondness for **cliché** (n.), phrases so overused they had lost their color entirely. Still, certain **maxims** (n.) endured — short, hard truths passed down like heirlooms — and the philosopher insisted every child learn at least one guiding **precept** (n.) before leaving home. His rivals, less kind, called his gentler sayings mere **bromide** (n.), pleasant noise dressed up as wisdom.

**dictum** *(n.)*
- Meaning: A formal or authoritative saying or pronouncement
- বাংলা অর্থ: উক্তি, বাণী
- Feeling/Tone: Weighty, declarative, respected
- Texture: Solid, carved, lasting
- Usage: "The judge's dictum became a guiding principle for later cases."
- Etymology: Latin *dictum* — "thing said" (from *dicere*, "to say")
- Synonyms: pronouncement, maxim, saying
- Antonyms: query, question

**platitude** *(n.)*
- Meaning: A trite, overused remark, especially one offered as if profound
- বাংলা অর্থ: গতানুগতিক উক্তি
- Feeling/Tone: Flat, hollow, well-meaning but empty
- Texture: Worn, thin, smooth from overuse
- Usage: "His speech was full of platitudes and short on substance."
- Etymology: French *plat* — "flat"
- Synonyms: cliché, bromide, truism
- Antonyms: insight, revelation

**aphorism** *(n.)*
- Meaning: A concise statement expressing a general truth
- বাংলা অর্থ: সংক্ষিপ্ত জ্ঞানগর্ভ উক্তি
- Feeling/Tone: Sharp, wise, compact
- Texture: Crisp, polished, dense
- Usage: "Her aphorisms were often quoted long after the lecture ended."
- Etymology: Greek *aphorismos* — "definition, distinction"
- Synonyms: maxim, adage, epigram
- Antonyms: platitude (when hollow), ramble

**cliché** *(n.)*
- Meaning: An expression so overused it has lost originality or force
- বাংলা অর্থ: বহুব্যবহৃত অর্থহীন উক্তি
- Feeling/Tone: Tired, predictable, faintly irritating
- Texture: Flat, faded, stale
- Usage: "The film's dialogue leaned on every romantic cliché imaginable."
- Etymology: French *clicher* — printing term for a stereotype plate
- Synonyms: platitude, bromide, truism
- Antonyms: originality, novelty

**maxim** *(n.)*
- Meaning: A short statement expressing a general truth or rule of conduct
- বাংলা অর্থ: নীতিবাক্য
- Feeling/Tone: Firm, guiding, timeless
- Texture: Solid, compact, enduring
- Usage: "'Honesty is the best policy' is a maxim taught early in life."
- Etymology: Latin *maxima (propositio)* — "greatest premise"
- Synonyms: precept, dictum, aphorism
- Antonyms: falsehood, fallacy

**precept** *(n.)*
- Meaning: A general rule intended to guide behavior or morals
- বাংলা অর্থ: নীতি, উপদেশ
- Feeling/Tone: Instructive, formal, moral
- Texture: Structured, rigid, foundational
- Usage: "He lived by the precept that honesty must come before comfort."
- Etymology: Latin *praeceptum* — "thing taught, maxim"
- Synonyms: principle, tenet, rule
- Antonyms: exception, deviation

**bromide** *(n.)*
- Meaning: A trite or dull remark meant to soothe, lacking real insight
- বাংলা অর্থ: গতানুগতিক সান্ত্বনাসূচক উক্তি
- Feeling/Tone: Placid, tired, softly dismissive
- Texture: Bland, muted, sedative
- Usage: "Instead of real advice, she offered only bromides."
- Etymology: From the sedative drug bromide, extended metaphorically to dull speech
- Synonyms: platitude, cliché, truism
- Antonyms: insight, revelation

# LESSON 104
**Getting Better or Getting Worse?**

The storm had raged for three days before it finally began to **abate** (v.), the wind loosening its grip on the coastal town. But relief was brief — a broken levee would soon **exacerbate** (v.) the damage already done, flooding streets that had barely begun to dry. The mayor's speech, meant to reassure the frightened crowd, was **truncated** (v.) by a sudden power outage, leaving half his words lost to the dark. Meanwhile tensions between neighbors began to **escalate** (v.), each blaming the other for unrepaired drains. Officials tried to **curtail** (v.) the panic by rationing supplies fairly, and volunteers worked through the night to **assuage** (v.) the fear settling over the town. Slowly, aid began to **augment** (v.) what little the town had left, and by the week's end came the long-awaited **cessation** (n.) of rain — the first true stillness the sky had offered in days.

- Meaning: To become less intense; to diminish
- বাংলা অর্থ: হ্রাস পাওয়া, প্রশমিত হওয়া

- Texture: Ebbing, loosening
- Usage: "The fever finally abated by morning."
- Etymology: Latin *battuere* — "to beat down"

**exacerbate** *(v.)*
- Meaning: To make a bad situation worse

**curtail** *(v.)*

- Etymology: Latin *curtus* — "shortened"
- Synonyms: reduce, limit, truncate

**assuage** *(v.)*
- Meaning: To make an unpleasant feeling less intense; to soothe
- বাংলা অর্থ: প্রশমিত করা, সান্ত্বনা দেওয়া

**augment** *(v.)*

**cessation** *(n.)*

- Texture: Still, settled
- Usage: "The treaty led to a cessation of hostilities."
- Etymology: Latin *cessare* — "to cease, delay"
- Synonyms: halt, end, stoppage
- Antonyms: continuation, escalation, commencement

# LESSON 105
**It's All About the Self**

At the reunion dinner, the family's dynamics played out like a study in vanity and virtue. The eldest cousin, always **self-aggrandizing** (adj.), retold his modest business success as though he'd built an empire. His sister, by contrast, was so **self-deprecating** (adj.) about her own achievements that guests had to coax her praise out of politeness alone. Their grandmother watched quietly, her whole life a lesson in being **altruistic** (adj.) — she had given away more than she'd ever kept for herself. She remained **modest** (adj.) even when praised, deflecting every compliment with a wave of her hand. Her late husband had been the same, famously **selfless** (adj.), the kind of man who fed strangers before his own children. It was a strange thing, then, that their youngest grandson had grown so **egotistical** (adj.), boasting at every turn while barely listening to anyone else. The grandmother sighed and hoped he might yet learn to be **self-effacing** (adj.) like his grandfather, rather than mistaking humility for **self-abnegation** (n.), the total erasure of oneself that had once nearly broken her own mother's spirit.

**self-aggrandizing** *(adj.)*
- Meaning: Portraying oneself as more important or impressive than warranted
- বাংলা অর্থ: আত্মপ্রচারমূলক, নিজেকে বড় করে দেখানো
- Feeling/Tone: Boastful, inflated, transparent
- Texture: Puffed-up, hollow
- Usage: "His self-aggrandizing speech left the audience skeptical."
- Etymology: Latin *grandis* — "large"
- Synonyms: boastful, pompous, vainglorious
- Antonyms: modest, self-effacing, humble
